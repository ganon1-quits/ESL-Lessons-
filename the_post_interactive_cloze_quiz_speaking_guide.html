Introductory Quiz: Media, Law & Government Secrecy

Warm-Up Overview: Test your background knowledge of democratic principles, freedom of the press, and whistleblowing before exploring Steven Spielberg's The Post (2017).

📜 Historical Background & Movie Context

The Film: Directed by Steven Spielberg (2017) and starring Meryl Streep as publisher Katharine Graham and Tom Hanks as editor Ben Bradlee, The Post dramatizes the 1971 true story surrounding the publication of the Pentagon Papers.

  • What were the Pentagon Papers? A top-secret 7,000-page US Department of Defense study commissioned by Defense Secretary Robert McNamara, detailing 20 years of classified military and political involvement in Vietnam (1945–1967).
  • The Whistleblower: Military analyst Daniel Ellsberg secretly photocopied the study and leaked it to the press after realizing the US government was deceiving the public about the progress of the war.
  • The Government Deception: The documents proved that four successive presidential administrations (Truman, Eisenhower, Kennedy, and Johnson) systematically lied to Congress and citizens—privately admitting the Vietnam War was unwinnable while publicly sending thousands of troops into battle.
  • The Constitutional Crisis: When President Richard Nixon's White House obtained a federal court injunction to censor The New York Times, The Washington Post stepped in, risking corporate collapse and federal espionage charges to publish the files and trigger a landmark First Amendment battle at the US Supreme Court (*New York Times Co. v. United States*).
