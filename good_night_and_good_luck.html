Good Night, and Good Luck

ESL Current Events & Media Rhetoric Study Guide (B2–C1)

Segment 1: The Speech & The Spark (0:00 – 20:00)

Plot Summary: Edward R. Murrow gives his famous 1958 speech warning about the decay of television. The story flashes back to 1953 as the team investigates Milo Radulovich, an Air Force reservist discharged without trial due to political suspicions about his family.

Target Vocabulary

Sensationalism

noun

The use of shocking, exaggerated, or emotional language and images to provoke public interest.

Scapegoat

noun

A person or group made to bear the blame or punishment for others.

Subversive

adjective

Intended to undermine or overthrow an established political system or government.

Reservist

noun

A member of a military reserve force who is called upon in emergencies.

Hearsay

noun

Information received from other people that one cannot adequately substantiate; rumor.

Vocabulary Check

1. What does the term "sensationalism" mean in news media?

2. Complete the sentence: "The military used the young airman as a ________ to satisfy political pressure."

3. True or False: A "subversive" action is designed to support and protect existing government authority.

4. What is a "reservist" in a military context?

5. True or False: "Hearsay" is considered solid, direct evidence in a court of law.

Segment 2: Taking the Stand (20:00 – 38:00)

Plot Summary: Murrow and producer Fred Friendly face resistance from corporate bosses and advertisers who fear financial fallout. Despite the risks, they air the Radulovich broadcast, challenging Senator McCarthy's methods.

Target Vocabulary

Censorship

noun

The suppression or prohibition of speech or public communication considered objectionable.

Editorial

noun / adj

An article or statement expressing the official opinion or stance of the editor or publishers.

Sponsorship

noun

Financial support provided by a commercial sponsor or corporation in exchange for advertising.

Fallout

noun

The adverse results or negative consequences of a situation or action.

Affiliate

noun

A local broadcasting station associated with or affiliated to a larger national network.

Vocabulary Check

1. Which situation represents "censorship"?

2. Complete the sentence: "The network feared losing corporate ________ if they aired controversial political topics."

3. True or False: An "editorial" represents a news organization's opinion rather than pure neutral reporting.

4. What does "fallout" mean in the context of corporate decision-making?

5. Complete the sentence: "Local network ________ across the country were nervous about airing the program."

Segment 3: The Retaliation & The Hearing (38:00 – 55:00)

Plot Summary: Senator McCarthy counter-attacks on air, accusing Murrow of communist sympathies. Meanwhile, the news team covers Senate subcommittee hearings, exposing how accusations are made without formal proof.

Target Vocabulary

Defamation

noun

The act of damaging the good reputation of someone through false statements.

Cross-examine

verb

To question a witness called by an opposing party during a trial or hearing to test credibility.

Retaliation

noun

The act of returning an attack or responding to an action with a counter-attack.

Subpoena

noun / verb

A legal writ ordering a person to attend court or produce evidence under penalty.

Accusation

noun

A charge or claim that someone has done something illegal or wrong.

Vocabulary Check

1. True or False: Publishing false information that destroys a public figure's reputation is known as defamation.

2. What does it mean to "cross-examine" someone during a hearing?

3. Complete the sentence: "McCarthy’s speech on television was a direct ________ against Murrow’s previous broadcast."

4. What is a "subpoena"?

5. Complete the sentence: "The senator made a serious ________ without providing physical proof."

Segment 4: Murrow’s Rebuttal (55:00 – 1:12:00)

Plot Summary: Murrow delivers a carefully constructed rebuttal to McCarthy's claims on live television. He analyzes McCarthy's logical fallacies, emphasizing the distinction between political dissent and treason.

Target Vocabulary

Rebuttal

noun

A speech or written response presenting counter-evidence to disprove opposing claims.

Demagogue

noun

A political leader who appeals to popular desires and fears rather than rational argument.

Insinuation

noun

An unpleasant hint or indirect suggestion of something bad without stating it directly.

Fallacy

noun

A mistaken belief or a flaw in reasoning that invalidates an argument.

Rhetoric

noun

The art of effective or persuasive speaking or writing, especially using figures of speech.

Vocabulary Check

1. Which of the following best describes a "demagogue"?

2. Complete the sentence: "Murrow’s response was not just an apology; it was a powerful, point-by-point ________."

3. True or False: An "insinuation" is a direct, explicit statement backed by physical evidence.

4. What is a logical "fallacy"?

5. Complete the sentence: "Murrow used powerful political ________ to persuade his television audience."

Segment 5: The Aftermath & Legacy (1:12:00 – End)

Plot Summary: McCarthy's political influence crumbles following Senate investigations. However, executive pressure leads to See It Now losing its prime-time slot. Murrow completes his 1958 speech, delivering a timeless warning regarding broadcast media.

Target Vocabulary

Complacency

noun

A feeling of quiet satisfaction or security, often while unaware of potential danger.

Dissent

noun / verb

The expression or holding of opinions that differ from official policies or majority views.

Integrity

noun

The quality of being honest and adhering to strong moral principles.

Predecessor

noun

A person or thing that held a position or job before another.

Treason

noun

The crime of betraying one's country, especially by attempting to overthrow the government.

Vocabulary Check

1. What does Murrow warn against when he criticizes public "complacency"?

2. True or False: "Dissent" means complete agreement with majority political opinion.

3. Complete the sentence: "Despite commercial pressure, the journalists fought to maintain their professional ________."

4. What is a "predecessor"?

5. Complete the sentence: "Murrow argued that political disagreement must not be confused with ________ or disloyalty."
